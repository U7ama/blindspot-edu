Today we are comparing recipes using ratios. A recipe with two cups of flour for one cup of milk has a flour-to-milk ratio of two to one. Another recipe with four cups of flour for two cups of milk has the same ratio. We use equivalent fractions to compare the recipes.

When both quantities increase by the same factor, the ratio remains unchanged. Doubling both quantities lets us make a larger batch while preserving the proportions. If we doubled only the flour, the proportions would change. That is why scaling a recipe requires multiplying every ingredient by the same factor.

Now consider three cups of flour and two cups of milk. This recipe is different from the first recipe. We can compare the flour-to-milk ratios to describe the difference. Think about what equivalent fractions would tell you about these comparisons.
